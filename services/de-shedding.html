Quick answer

De-shedding is a bath and thorough brush-out that removes loose undercoat before it ends up on your floors and furniture. The Pet-Stetician does it one-on-one in a mobile grooming van at your home in Palm Beach, Martin, and St. Lucie Counties.

Why Florida dogs shed so much

Many dogs blow their coat seasonally, but in Florida’s warm climate shedding can feel like it never stops. Double-coated breeds hold onto a dense undercoat that, if it isn’t removed, traps heat and moisture against the skin.

Who needs de-shedding

  • Golden retrievers and Labs
  • German shepherds, huskies, and other double-coated breeds
  • Corgis, Australian shepherds, and herding breeds
  • Any dog leaving tumbleweeds of fur around the house

Keeping shedding under control at home

Brushing a few times a week with the right brush for your dog’s coat, plus a professional de-shed every 4–8 weeks, keeps the undercoat from building up. Ask Julia which brush suits your dog.

Good to Know

De-Shedding FAQ

Should I shave my double-coated dog to stop shedding?

Usually not. A double coat helps protect your dog from heat and sun, and shaving can affect how it grows back. Regular de-shedding removes the loose undercoat without taking away that protection.

How often should I book de-shedding?

Every 4–8 weeks is typical, and more often during heavy shedding seasons.
